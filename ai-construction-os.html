SH-01

The spine

Every stage runs its own agents, but none of them holds the truth. Each stage publishes its events onto one bus and takes its instructions back from one place. That single loop — not the individual models — is what makes this an operating system rather than twelve disconnected pilots.

SH-02

The core loop

The same nine steps run behind every stage — a cement pour, a vendor award, a lead follow-up. What changes stage to stage is only the data and the autonomy level. The gate at step 7 is the load-bearing element: anything that spends money, signs a document, stops work or touches a customer contract must physically pass through a named human.

01 → 02

Data & monitor

Cameras, drones, IoT meters, phone photos, ERP rows, portal statuses and documents land on one bus in a common schema. Nothing is "in someone's WhatsApp".

03 → 04

Analyze & predict

Actual is compared to plan, model, code and contract. Then the system extrapolates: finish date, cost at completion, defect likelihood, absorption — each with a confidence band, never a lone number.

05 → 06

Alert & recommend

An alert without an owner is noise. Every alert carries a severity, an owner, an SLA and the evidence frame. Every recommendation carries at least two options and their rupee and day impact.

07 → 09

Approve, execute, learn

A human signs. The system then writes the change back into P6, the ERP, the PO, the CRM — and records what actually happened so the next prediction is better calibrated.

SH-03

The command center

Ten departments that today argue about whose spreadsheet is right. One layer that reads from all of them, writes back to all of them, and keeps a signed record of who decided what. The value is not the models — it is that legal, design, cost, site, sales and facilities are finally looking at the same object.

SH-04

The twelve stages

Each sheet below lists the agents actually on shift, what they read, what they hand forward, and the one human whose signature the stage cannot proceed without. Autonomy levels are defined in SH-08.

Land

Find, price and title-check parcels faster than the market — and walk away from bad ones before the diligence bill arrives.

02

Feasibility & ROI

Turn a buildable envelope into a defensible number — and show the downside honestly enough that the investment committee can actually say no.

L2 · auto-draft

Agents on shift · 4

  • Massing Optimizer — generates dozens of compliant massing options across the FSI envelope, trading saleable area against unit mix, parking, daylight, views and podium efficiency, and returns the Pareto front rather than one architect's favourite.
  • Financial Model Agent — builds the full cashflow: land, approvals, construction, finance, marketing and overhead against a modelled sales curve. Outputs IRR, NPV, peak equity, breakeven sales percentage and payback.
  • Demand & Absorption Agent — estimates realistic ₹/sq ft and monthly absorption per unit type from registry data, competing launches and inventory overhang in the micro-market.
  • Risk Simulator — Monte Carlo across cost inflation, approval delay, interest rate and sales slowdown; reports P10/P50/P90 IRR and the month at which equity runs out in the downside case.

BOQ, estimation & cost control

SH-05

How the site is actually watched

Cameras alone produce footage nobody reviews. The value is in the four-step chain: capture everything, turn pixels and readings into detections, turn detections into typed events with an owner and a clock, and then make something happen — including blocking the activity downstream.

Privacy is a design constraint

Cameras on a site watch workers. Publish the monitoring policy, restrict face-level identification to the gate, keep worker-level productivity aggregated to the gang, retain footage on a stated clock, and involve the labour contractor before the first camera goes up. Do this badly and the cameras get covered with cement bags in week two.

Connectivity, honestly

Sites lose network. Inference runs at the edge on a small box in the site office; only detections and thumbnails are synced, not raw video. The system must degrade to store-and-forward rather than stop.

Dust, night and monsoon

Vision accuracy drops in dust, glare, rain and floodlight. Report confidence with every detection, route low-confidence frames to a human queue instead of the alert stream, and never let a vision output become a payment trigger without a human measurement check.

SH-08

How much the system is allowed to do

Every agent carries an autonomy level. The level is a property of the action, not of how good the model is — a highly accurate model still may not sign a cheque. Raise a level only after the agent has a measured track record at the level below.

L0
Observe

Collects and displays. Makes no claim about what should happen.

gate RFID counts
meter readings
document register
L1
Advise

Surfaces a finding or a ranking. A human does all the work of acting on it.

vendor reliability score
land parcel shortlist
VE alternates
L2
Auto-draft

Produces the finished artefact — the application pack, the comparative statement, the demand letter — and a human reviews and signs it. This is where most of this system lives.

approval submission packs
BOQ and rate analysis
RFQs and comparisons
feasibility models
demand letters
L3
Auto-execute with veto

Acts immediately, notifies the owner, and stays reversible for a stated window. Used where the cost of waiting exceeds the cost of being wrong.

block a dependent activity
publish the 3-week look-ahead
update % complete
assign a snag with SLA
raise a preventive work order
L4
Fully automatic

No human in the path, because there is no judgement in the path.

ingestion and normalisation
alert routing
report generation
portal status polling

The capped-at-L2 rule

Any action that spends money, signs or files a document, stops work, changes a contract price or makes a commitment to a customer is capped at L2 permanently. No accuracy score unlocks it. This is what makes the system safe to deploy quickly everywhere else.

Earning a level

An agent moves from L1 to L2 after 90 days where its drafts were accepted with minor edits 85%+ of the time. L2 to L3 requires a measured false-positive rate below the agreed threshold and a working reversal path that someone has actually tested.

Falling back

Levels drop automatically. Three overturned L3 actions in a rolling month, or a confidence distribution that shifts beyond its band, demotes the agent to L2 until it is re-reviewed. Silent drift is the failure mode that kills trust.

THE ONE HARD REQUIREMENT

A single element identity

Column C-14 on the 7th floor must be the same object in the model, the BOQ line, the pour record, the cube test, the camera detection, the snag list and the maintenance ticket. Every capability on this page collapses into disconnected demos without it, and no amount of model quality substitutes for it.

WRITE DISCIPLINE

Every write is attributable

When the system updates a percentage, a bill line or a schedule date, the record carries which agent wrote it, from which evidence, under which autonomy level, and who could reverse it. This is what makes the output usable in a contractual dispute rather than a liability in one.

FALLBACK

Degrade, never stop

Site loses network, a portal changes its HTML, a camera goes down. Each integration declares its staleness tolerance; past it, the dashboard shows the field as stale rather than showing an old number as if it were current. A confidently wrong dashboard is worse than a blank one.

SH-10

How to actually get there

Building all twelve stages at once is how these programmes die. Sequence by where the money and the evidence are: sense first, because it is the cheapest way to prove the system tells the truth; automate the front office last, because it is the least forgiving of a wrong answer.

Phase 0
Months 1–3
The data spine

One element identity, one event bus, connectors to ERP, BIM, P6 and CRM. Nothing user-facing ships. This phase is unglamorous, unavoidable, and the single best predictor of whether the rest works.

Phase 1
Months 3–7
Sense the site — S-07, S-08, S-09

Cameras, one drone cadence, gate RFID, the safety and progress agents, and the command screen. Run it alongside the existing weekly report for two months and let the two disagree in public. Credibility is earned here or not at all.

Phase 2
Months 6–11
Control the money — S-05, S-06

Model-driven take-off, rate analysis, live forecast at completion, vendor scoring and normalised comparisons. This is where the programme pays for itself, and it only works because Phase 1 made the progress numbers trustworthy.

Phase 3
Months 10–16
Front of house — S-10, S-11

Lead scoring, the conversational agent, the customer progress portal fed by verified progress, snag scanning and the digital home file. The portal is only safe to launch once the progress number behind it is the measured one.

Phase 4
Months 14–24
Close the loop — S-01 to S-04, S-12

Land scouting, title reading, feasibility, approvals tracking and predictive maintenance. By now the system has enough completed history to make its own predictions calibrated instead of theoretical, and maintenance failures start feeding back into design.

SH-11

Where this breaks

Every honest system document has this section. These are the failure modes that have killed comparable programmes, and the design choice on this page that is meant to survive each one.

Alert fatigue

A vision model firing two hundred times a day gets muted in a fortnight. Mitigation: severity tiers, deduplication against open events, an owner and SLA on every alert, and a threshold that tightens automatically as dismissal rate rises.

Garbage BOQ coding

If the model is not coded to the schedule of rates, take-off produces confident nonsense. Mitigation: a modelling standard enforced at consultant appointment, and a reconciliation report that refuses to publish quantities below a coverage threshold.

Vendors gaming the score

Any published score gets optimised for. Mitigation: weight outcome data the vendor cannot author — measured delivery dates, rejection rates at the gate, safety incidents — over anything self-reported.

Legal exposure on documents

An AI-assembled submission that is wrong is still the developer's submission. Mitigation: the hard rule at G4 — nothing is filed unsigned — plus a completeness check that reports what it could not verify, not just what it could.

Worker resistance and privacy

Cameras aimed at people generate resentment and legal risk. Mitigation: a published monitoring policy, gang-level rather than individual productivity, face identification restricted to the gate, a stated retention clock, and the labour contractor consulted before installation.

Model drift after handover

A defect model trained on Tower A degrades on a different façade system. Mitigation: confidence monitoring per project, automatic demotion of autonomy level on drift, and the label store from SH-05 feeding a scheduled retrain.

The dashboard nobody opens

The most common ending. Mitigation: push the three things that need a decision to the phone of the person who has to decide, and judge the system on gate response times rather than on dashboard logins.

Over-trusting a single number

"The AI says 58%" becomes a payment trigger and then a dispute. Mitigation: every measurement carries a confidence band and its source frame, and a human measurement check stays in the loop for anything that releases money.
