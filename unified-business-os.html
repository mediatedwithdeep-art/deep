01

Why these two belong on one system

Real estate and solar EPC have different physics. A flat is one transaction with a long tail of paperwork; a rooftop plant is a small project with procurement, labour and a 25-year promise. What they share is everything expensive: the person, the money, and your attention.

Shared asset 01

One person, two needs

A buyer who registers a 3 BHK will pay an electricity bill in that flat within ninety days. A factory owner who signs a 150 kW rooftop is a person who buys industrial land. Two CRMs make that invisible. One record makes it a queue.

Shared asset 02

One month's cash

Brokerage lands in lumps; EPC eats working capital in advance against a milestone you have not billed yet. Separate books mean you find out on the 28th. One ledger means the system tells you on the 8th which solar PO the brokerage is actually funding.

Shared asset 03

One queue of attention

You cannot be in two pipelines. The point of the command center is not more dashboards — it is a single ranked list of the things only you can decide, with everything else already drafted and waiting for a yes.

02

The loop the whole business runs on

Ten stages, one direction, and it does not end at the sale. Stages 8–10 are where a one-deal business becomes a compounding one: the same customer, acquired once, sold twice, and turned into the cheapest lead source you will ever have.

The rule that makes the loop real: a stage may only advance when its exit condition is recorded as data — a score, a signed file, a payment, a meter reading. “I spoke to him, he sounded positive” is not an exit condition, and an AI that accepts it will quietly fill your pipeline with ghosts.

05

How much each agent is allowed to do

Autonomy is a setting, not a personality. Every agent starts at L1 and earns its way up on evidence. Nothing that touches money, law or a promise to a customer ever goes above L2.

L0 — Observe

Reads, reports, never acts

It builds the view and hands it to a person. The negotiation coach lives here permanently, because the thing it advises on is judgement.

L1 — Draft

Prepares work, a human sends it

Quotes, designs, BOQs, documents, ad copy. The agent does ninety per cent of the effort and none of the committing. Every new agent starts here.

L2 — Act within limits

Acts inside a written boundary

A named template list, a value cap, a time window, a message ceiling. Everything is logged and reversible, and stepping outside the boundary is an escalation, not a judgement call.

L3 — Act and report

Runs its domain, reviewed weekly

Only for work where a mistake costs a correction, not a rupee or a reputation: routing, matching, task-keeping, reading a bill, building a dashboard.

Promotion

Earning a level

An agent moves up after a fixed run — say fifty decisions — where the human approved its draft essentially unchanged. The number is the evidence; a feeling that “it seems good now” is not.

Demotion

Falling back

One customer-visible error, one margin breach, or one compliance miss drops the agent a level automatically. It climbs back the same way it climbed the first time.

Ceiling

The permanent cap

Money out, a signature, a statutory filing, a price below the floor, and first contact across rails stay at L1 forever. No amount of good behaviour unlocks them.

06

The cross-sell engine

This is the only section that could not exist if the two businesses ran on separate systems. A cross-sell is not a broadcast — it is a specific signal, a specific window, and one message. Get the timing wrong and you spend the trust you earned on the first deal.

DirectionWhat the system seesWhen it fires The offerWhy it converts
A → B Flat registered, possession taken, first bill above roughly 250 units a month 30–90 days after the first bill Rooftop sized to that bill, subsidy applied, instalment set near what they already pay the DISCOM The bill is fresh, the flat is still half-empty, and you are the person who handed them the keys

B → A Residential solar customer, plant performing at or above the promise 12 months after commissioning Investment property or plot — positioned as the next asset, not the next purchase You have twelve months of delivering exactly what you said you would. That is the entire pitch

Suppression rules

When the engine must stay quiet

No cross-sell while a ticket or complaint is open on the first deal. None until the first deal is fully delivered — possession taken or plant exporting. Never in the same message as a referral ask. At most one attempt per customer per six months, and if they say no, the record says no for a year.

Approval

A human opens every crossing

The matchmaker builds the queue and drafts the opening line; a person reads the name, remembers the deal, and either sends it or kills it. This is deliberately the slowest step in the system. A wrongly-timed cross-sell costs a customer, and customers here are worth more than any single order.

On the numbers: the windows above are starting positions, not findings. Run the engine for two quarters with the timing logged, then replace every figure in this table with what your own data says — especially the 250-unit threshold and the 30-90 day window, which will move with the locality and the season.

Nine gates. They are not there because the AI is unreliable — they are there because these are the decisions that are expensive to reverse, and because your name, not the system's, is on the document.

09

What actually makes it one system

Not the dashboard. Three unglamorous things underneath it, and if any one of them is wrong the rest of this document is decoration.

Requirement 01

One identity per human

  • The person is the root record. Deals hang off them, not the other way round.
  • Phone number is the key, normalised to one format, matched before anything else happens.
  • A person can hold a property deal and a solar deal at once without becoming two customers.
  • Family and company links are explicit — the man who bought the flat and the firm that bought the plant may be the same wallet.

Requirement 02

One identity per place

  • A flat, a plot, a factory roof and a shed are all sites: an address, a geocode, an owner link.
  • A property you sold and a roof you later fit are the same site, with two deals against it.
  • This is what lets the system notice, unprompted, that the roof it just surveyed is a building it sold.
  • Without it, cross-sell is a manual memory exercise and stays one forever.

Requirement 03

One ledger, both rails

  • Every rupee carries a rail, a deal, a milestone and a date it is expected on.
  • Brokerage received and a module PO due sit in the same 30-day view, not two apps.
  • Subsidy and retention are receivables with their own ageing, never treated as arrived.
  • Margin is computed from actuals after the job, not from the estimate that won it.
Discipline 01

Every write is signed

Each change carries who or what made it, when, and on what basis. When an agent is wrong, you need to find every record it touched in one query — not reconstruct it from memory.

Discipline 02

Degrade, never drop

If the messaging API is down, the bill is unreadable or a vendor portal is offline, the work falls into a human queue with the reason attached. The one unacceptable outcome is a lead or a commitment quietly disappearing.

Discipline 03

Rules are config, not code

Subsidy rates, tariff slabs, margin floors, cadence ceilings and PO thresholds live in one table with a “verified on” date. Anything that changes by government notification must be changeable by you in a minute.

How to actually get there

Six months, six phases, in this order. The sequence matters more than the speed: every phase depends on the one before it being genuinely finished, and the cross-sell engine — the reason for the whole exercise — comes last because it needs everything else to be true first.

WEEKS 1–2Phase 0

One record

  • Both businesses migrated into one customer database, phone number as the key
  • Duplicates merged by hand — this is tedious and it is the whole foundation
  • Sites created for every property sold and every plant installed to date
  • No AI yet. Deliberately
Live at the end: you can answer “have we ever dealt with this number, on either side?” in one search.
WEEKS 3–6Phase 1

Capture and conversation

  • Every channel routed into one inbox; router live at L3, qualifier at L1
  • WhatsApp Business Platform connected with an approved template library
  • Rail-specific qualification question sets written and tested on real leads
  • Call transcription writing onto the record
Live at the end: no lead is unanswered for more than a minute, and every lead has a score with a reason.
WEEKS 7–10Phase 2

The quote engine

  • Rate library built for both rails, with margin floors written down for the first time
  • Bill analyst live; sizing and BOQ agents drafting at L1
  • Property matching live with reasons attached to every recommendation
  • Gates G2, G3 and G4 enforced from day one of this phase
Live at the end: a quote takes minutes instead of a day, and you know the margin before you send it.
WEEKS 11–14Phase 3

Money

  • One ledger across both rails; milestone invoicing and collections automated to L2
  • Subsidy and retention tracked as ageing receivables
  • Command screen v1 — the approvals queue and the 30-day cash view, nothing else
Live at the end: on the 8th you know whether the month works, and which order is funding which.
WEEKS 15–20Phase 4

Execution

Where this breaks

Eight failure modes, ranked by how quietly they happen. None of them are exotic. Every one has been the reason a system like this got abandoned somewhere.

Highest risk

Dirty data at the start

Two rails, two spellings, one phone number written three ways. An agent reasoning over that will produce fluent, confident, wrong answers — and they will look authoritative because they arrive in a dashboard. Fix: Phase 0 is not optional, and no agent goes live on a record set that has not been merged by hand.

Highest risk

A confidently misread bill

OCR reads 340 units as 40, the plant is sized at a third of what the customer needs, and you find out at the first bill after commissioning — when it is a 25-year problem. Fix: the bill analyst reports confidence, refuses low-confidence reads, and sizing is cross-checked against sanctioned load before any quote leaves.

Expensive

A subsidy that changed last month

Scheme rules, caps, approved-model lists and DISCOM procedures move by notification. A quote that promises the old number is a loss you have already committed to in writing. Fix: scheme rules live in config with a “verified on” date, and any quote built on a stale entry is blocked, not warned.

Expensive

A rate library nobody updates

Module, inverter, steel and cable prices move. A three-month-old rate library sells at a loss with total confidence, and the BOQ agent will not notice because it has no other truth. Fix: costing compares every job's actuals against its estimate, and a drift beyond a set threshold forces a library review before the next quote.

Expensive

Over-promised generation

A generous number in a quotation becomes a 25-year expectation, and the first cloudy month turns into a dispute. Fix: quote conservatively with the losses stated, record actual generation against the promise on every plant, and let the support agent surface the gap before the customer does.

Expensive

Cross-sell spam

The engine works so well that it gets pointed at everyone, and the customers who would have referred you stop replying. The cost never shows up as a line item — it shows up as a pipeline that stops filling itself. Fix: the suppression rules in section 06 are hard limits, not defaults, and G8 stays at L1 permanently.

Structural

One person holding nine gates

Every approval routes to the founder, the founder travels, and the whole system's throughput becomes one inbox. The team then works around it, and the records stop being true. Fix: every gate gets a named deputy and a response-time target, and the command screen shows what waiting is costing.

Structural

The screen nobody opens

It happens the moment the screen shows numbers instead of decisions. A dashboard that reports is a dashboard you stop visiting; a queue that blocks work is one you clear daily. Fix: the command center opens on the approvals queue, and any metric that has not changed a decision in a quarter comes off it.

Messaging and calling rules are a live constraint, not a footnote. Outbound WhatsApp runs on approved templates with a quality rating you can lose; outbound calling is bound by consent and calling-preference regulation; property advertising carries its own disclosure requirements. Build the cadence ceilings and the disclosure fields in from Phase 1 — retrofitting compliance onto a running automation is far more painful than designing with it.

The one sentence version

Acquire the customer once. Sell them twice. Let them bring the third.

Everything above is machinery for that sentence. The agents exist so the second sale costs a phone call instead of an ad budget. The gates exist so the second sale never costs you the first customer. The shared spine exists because the moment that makes a cross-sell possible — a first electricity bill, a sentence said to an engineer on a roof — is invisible to a business that runs two systems.

Build Phase 0 properly, keep the nine gates honest, and replace every placeholder number in this document with a measured one by the end of the second quarter. That is the whole plan.
